2. SUITABILITY SCORE FORMULA
----------------------------

Each crop receives four normalized components:

  water_fit  = 1 - irrigation_deficit_ratio
  stress_fit = clamp(root_zone_wetness * 1.5, 0, 1)
  soil_fit   = 1.0 when AWC is unavailable; otherwise clamp(AWC / 30, 0, 1)
  slope_fit  = 0.9 when slope >= 15%; otherwise 1.0

The score is:

  score = 100 * (
      0.45 * water_fit
    + 0.25 * stress_fit
    + 0.20 * soil_fit
    + 0.10 * slope_fit
  )

The score is clamped to 0-100.

Water calculations:

  crop_ETc_mm = ET0_mean_mm_per_day * growing_days * Kc_mid
  rainfall_available_mm = annual_rainfall_mm * growing_days / 365
  irrigation_mm = max(0, crop_ETc_mm - rainfall_available_mm)
  irrigation_m3_per_ha = irrigation_mm * 10
  deficit_ratio = irrigation_mm / crop_ETc_mm

Yield factor:

  heat_penalty = min(0.35, heat_days / growing_days * 1.5)
  yield_factor = max(
      0,
      1 - Ky * deficit_ratio - heat_penalty
  )

Revenue:

  estimated_revenue_usd_per_ha =
      yield_t_per_ha * price_usd_per_t * yield_factor

Fertilizer savings:

  fertilizer_saving_usd_per_ha =
      max(0, nitrogen_contribution_kg_per_ha * 1.20)

The nitrogen value is a reference assumption of 1.20 USD per kg.

4.1 Grain Corn
--------------

Traits:
  Kc_mid:                         1.20
  Growing duration:               135 days
  Water-stress sensitivity Ky:    1.25
  Nitrogen contribution:          -160 kg/ha
  Reference yield:                9.5 t/ha
  Reference price:                190 USD/t

Calculation:
  crop_ETc = 2.57 * 135 * 1.20
           = 416.34 mm

  rainfall_available = 1090.30 * 135 / 365
                     = 403.27 mm approximately

  irrigation = 416.34 - 403.27
             = 13.08 mm
             = 130.78 m3/ha

  water_fit = 1 - (13.08 / 416.34)
            = 0.969

  score = 100 * (
      0.45 * 0.969
    + 0.25 * 0.765
    + 0.20 * 1.000
    + 0.10 * 1.000
  )
        = 92.72 / 100

  yield_factor = 0.894
  revenue = 9.5 * 190 * 0.894
           = 1613.79 USD/ha
  fertilizer saving = max(0, -160 * 1.20)
                    = 0 USD/ha

Result:
  Suitability:       92.72 / 100
  Irrigation:        130.78 m3/ha
  Revenue estimate:  1613.79 USD/ha

Why it is not the top recommendation:
  Corn is commercially attractive, but it has the lowest suitability score
  in this Iowa run, requires the only modeled irrigation, and removes
  approximately 160 kg/ha of nitrogen.

5. WORST-TO-BEST RANKING
-------------------------

The Iowa scores are:

  1. Grain Corn              92.72
  2. Soybeans                94.14
  3. Winter Wheat            94.14
  4. Red Clover               94.14
  5. Winter Rye               94.14

The four-way tie is expected because the score formula is primarily
environmental. The engine selects Soybeans as the recommended crop because
it wins the secondary revenue tie-breaker while also contributing nitrogen
and fertilizer savings.

This does not mean soybeans are universally better. The best choice depends
on the user's objective:

  Highest environmental fit:      Soybeans, wheat, clover, or rye (tie)
  Direct market revenue:          Grain Corn in this reference model
  Nitrogen contribution:          Red Clover
  Soil-cover function:            Red Clover or Winter Rye
  Balanced cash and soil benefit: Soybeans


6. ROTATION STRATEGIES
----------------------

6.1 Baseline cereal
    Sequence: Corn -> Corn -> Corn
    Mean suitability:              92.72
    Water use:                     392.34 m3/ha
    Supplemental income:           4841.37 USD/ha
    Fertilizer savings:            0.00 USD/ha
    Net nitrogen contribution:     -480 kg/ha

    Advantage:
      Highest reference income in this model.

    Disadvantage:
      Highest water use, repeated nitrogen removal, and weakest long-term
      soil-balance profile.

6.2 Cereal-legume rotation
    Sequence: Corn -> Soybeans -> Winter Wheat
    Mean suitability:              93.67
    Water use:                     130.78 m3/ha
    Supplemental income:           4132.34 USD/ha
    Fertilizer savings:            48.00 USD/ha
    Net nitrogen contribution:     -230 kg/ha

    Advantage:
      Much lower water use than continuous corn and includes a legume.

    Disadvantage:
      Nitrogen balance remains negative because wheat and corn remove more
      nitrogen than soybeans contribute.

6.3 Adaptive cover rotation
    Sequence: Corn -> Soybeans -> Red Clover
    Mean suitability:              93.67
    Water use:                     130.78 m3/ha
    Supplemental income:           2886.59 USD/ha
    Fertilizer savings:            156.00 USD/ha
    Net nitrogen contribution:     -30 kg/ha

    Advantage:
      Same low modeled water use as the cereal-legume strategy, the highest
      fertilizer savings, and the closest nitrogen balance to neutral.

    Disadvantage:
      Lower direct income because red clover is modeled as a cover crop with
      no direct market revenue.


7. WHY THE ADAPTIVE COVER ROTATION IS BEST
------------------------------------------

The engine recommends the Adaptive cover rotation because it provides the
best combined resilience trade-off:

  - Mean suitability:              93.67
  - Water use:                     130.78 m3/ha
  - Fertilizer savings:            156.00 USD/ha
  - Nitrogen balance:              -30 kg/ha
  - Includes a legume:             Soybeans
  - Includes a cover crop:         Red Clover

It is not the highest-income strategy. Continuous corn produces more direct
reference income, but at the cost of much higher water use and a nitrogen
balance of -480 kg/ha. The adaptive strategy is better when resilience,
fertilizer savings, water reduction, and soil protection matter more than
maximum short-term revenue.


8. RECOMMENDATIONS
-------------------

For the current Iowa demo:

  1. Use Soybeans when a cash crop with good environmental fit and positive
     nitrogen contribution is desired.
  2. Use Red Clover as the cover-crop phase when fertilizer savings and
     nitrogen improvement are priorities.
  3. Prefer the Adaptive cover rotation for a balanced resilience plan.
  4. Use continuous corn only when maximum modeled direct income is the
     priority and water/fertilizer impacts are acceptable.
  5. Keep a legume in the sequence to reduce nitrogen pressure.
  6. Keep soil covered during bare-ground windows to reduce erosion and
     moisture loss.
  7. Recalculate before commercial decisions using local yields, prices,
     fertilizer prices, planting dates, and actual irrigation costs.
  8. Treat the current results as medium-confidence because SoilGrids did
     not return an available-water-capacity value for this run.


9. LIMITATIONS
--------------

  - Crop prices and yields are reference assumptions, not live local quotes.
  - Annual rainfall is apportioned across each crop's typical growing days.
  - The current crop score is a screening score, not a calibrated yield model.
  - The optical crop-health result uses the selected Sentinel-2 scene.
  - Soil AWC was unavailable in the documented Iowa run.
  - Cover-crop revenue is set to zero; ecosystem or forage value is not
    currently monetized.
  - Local agronomic advice should be reviewed before implementation.
